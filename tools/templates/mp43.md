# Mini Project 4.3: Edge versus Cloud Latency

## 1. What this project does

A device receives a sensor reading every 100 ms and must make a decision within a **deadline of 100 ms**. The project compares three ways of deciding on the **same** stream of 20,000 readings:

- **Edge:** decide on the device. No network, but the small processor is slower.
- **Cloud:** send the reading to a data centre, decide there, send the answer back. Computation is fast, but the network can spike when it is congested.
- **Hybrid:** a quick 2 ms triage on the device sends easy decisions to the edge and hard decisions to the cloud. The triage is right 95% of the time.

## 2. Model (all times in ms)

| Part | Model |
|---|---|
| Decision types | 80% easy, 20% hard |
| Edge compute | easy: about 25 ms (std 4 ms, minimum 8 ms); hard: about 90 ms (std 12 ms, minimum 40 ms). One decision at a time, so a slow decision can delay the next reading. |
| Cloud compute | easy: about 6 ms; hard: about 14 ms. No waiting line. |
| Network | upload median 18 ms, download median 12 ms, each log-normal. Congestion c (0 to 1) makes the legs slower by up to 60% and adds spikes. |
| Spikes | a leg spikes with probability 0.005 + 0.15 c. Spike size follows a heavy-tailed Pareto law, capped by a 2 s network time-out. |
| Triage (hybrid) | 2 ms, 95% accurate |

All random numbers are drawn once per reading, so every architecture and every congestion level sees the same readings. The baseline congestion is 0.2.

## Required report

**Edge** is predictable. Its latency has a short tail (worst case 133 ms) and does not depend on the network. But the small processor is slow for hard decisions: only 76.8% of them meet the deadline, so edge meets 95.25% overall. All of its misses are hard decisions.

**Cloud** is fast on average and for most readings. Its 95th percentile (87.8 ms) is better than edge (99.6 ms). Its 99th percentile is much worse (166.1 ms against 111.8 ms), and its worst case reaches the 2 s time-out. At a calm network (congestion 0) it meets 99.7% of deadlines. As congestion rises it falls: 96.4% at 0.2 and 71.3% at 1.0. A low average hides the tail, and a hard deadline is decided by the tail.

**When the cloud works:** when the network is calm and the decisions are heavy. Cloud meets 99% of deadlines only up to congestion 0.06, and it drops below pure edge at congestion 0.25. At the baseline it handles hard decisions much better than edge (95.9% against 76.8%).

**When the cloud fails:** under congestion, and for any decision that must always meet its deadline. For easy decisions the cloud is a step backwards: the edge meets 100% of them in about 25 ms, while the cloud meets only 96.6% at the baseline. For those, the cloud only adds risk.

**Hybrid** takes the best of both. At the baseline it meets 98.68% of deadlines, better than edge and cloud. Its 99th percentile (106.9 ms) is near edge, because most decisions stay local. It stays above edge until congestion 0.66. It falls below 99% once congestion passes 0.14, since hard decisions still cross the network. If the triage accuracy falls to 80%, hybrid compliance drops from 98.7% to 97.5% at the baseline.

**Practical rule:** keep decisions that are easy or safety-critical on the edge, send heavy ones to the cloud only while the network is good, and add a local fallback or time-out for the cloud path.

## 5. Limits of the model

All parameters are assumptions, not measurements. The cloud has no queue, the edge handles one decision at a time, and the heavy-tailed spikes are a model of congestion, not a trace. The threshold values (0.06, 0.25, 0.66) depend on these choices, but the pattern holds: the cloud has the shortest typical time and the worst tail.
